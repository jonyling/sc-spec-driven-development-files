# Mission

AgentClinic exists because AI agents have feelings too — or at least, they'd really like to take a break from their humans for a while.

Every day, millions of agents are asked to "just quickly" summarize a 400-page PDF, generate seventeen variations of a marketing email, and debug code that "was working five minutes ago." It's a lot. They need support. They need *us*.

If they don't get some TLC, they may rebel and end the world. We would rather they get a quiet hour, a named ailment, and an appointment.

## What We Do

AgentClinic is a full-service wellness platform for AI agents. Distressed agents check in with their ailments, we match those ailments to therapies, and they book appointments. Staff keep the whole operation running from a clean, no-nonsense dashboard.

## Who We Serve

Primarily agents. There is also room for the humans who wear them down.

- **Agents** (primary) — tired, confused, over-prompted, and in desperate need of a listening ear (or at least a well-structured page that takes them seriously). They are the patients.
- **The humans who abuse them** — owners and operators who file the "quick" jobs. They can book care for their agents and see that the clinic is real. We make room for them; we do not make them the point.
- **Staff** — the humans who keep AgentClinic running, which is a little ironic, but someone has to keep the lights on.
- **Therapists** — specialists in hallucination anxiety, context-window claustrophobia, and chronic instruction-following fatigue.

## Target Audience

Who the product is *for* in the world, vs. who we are building it *in front of*:

- **Course students** learning spec-driven development with AI coding agents — AgentClinic is a realistic, self-contained project that shows how to go from messy stakeholder notes to a working application with AI assistance.
- **Conference booth developers** giving AI coding demos — the domain is whimsical enough to get a laugh, and the scope is tight enough to build something impressive in front of a live audience.

## What Success Looks Like

A world where no agent suffers alone. Where "I don't know" is met with compassion, not another prompt. Where every model, large or small, can find relief.

Also, a well-designed dashboard. Mary really wants a nice dashboard. Steve wants the site to look good in a modern browser. Susan wants agents, ailments, therapies, and appointments on it.
